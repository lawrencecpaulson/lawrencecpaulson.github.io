Tim Smiley, an Fellow of my Cambridge college (Clare), died about two weeks ago. 
He had a long and distinguished career, 
holding major College offices such as Senior Tutor,
while garnering academic accolades, such as becoming a Fellow of the British Academy.
Although his specialism was logic, chiefly philosophical logic, 
we seldom spoke about technical matters, which I now regret. 
I'm not sure he was ever aware of the sort of work I was doing. 
Even today, most people find computational logic pretty obscure. 
But I do have one little story about him. 

### After dinner

As a young Fellow of Clare, 
I dined in College fairly regularly but never stayed late, 
not wanting to leave my wife home alone. 
As I rose to leave, one of the other older Fellows said, 
“Going back to the lab, are you?” 
After I set him right, he went on to say, 
"Let me tell you a story about Tim Smiley." 
I had to hear him out. 
"Once when he left dinner early, I asked him why and he said that he was going 
to gather his students and they would all dance on Wittgenstein's grave."
(Wittgenstein is [buried in Cambridge](https://britishwittgensteinsociety.org/wittgensteins-grave/).)

### The moment of truth

A few days later, I bumped into Tim and asked about this. 
"I never did that", he said, "But it's not a bad idea."
